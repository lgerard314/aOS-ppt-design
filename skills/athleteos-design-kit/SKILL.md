# AthleteOS design guidelines for PowerPoint

These are the brand's design rules, taken from the AthleteOS marketing website and translated into slide terms. Apply them to every slide you create or edit for AthleteOS, whatever the slide is about. They describe a system, not a set of templates: any page built from these colours, sizes, spacings and rules will look like AthleteOS.

Everything about colour, typefaces, shapes, photography and the logo is the website's own, value for value. The point sizes, inch measurements, page numbers, charts and tables are that system carried over to a slide, since the website has no slides. The pictures in `reference/site-screenshots/` are the website itself: look at them before designing, because a description undersells how large the type is and how much of the page is photograph.

Files beside this one: `logo/` (the wordmark and monogram), `fonts/` (the two typefaces, to install), `reference/site-screenshots/` (the source of the look).

## The look in one paragraph

Dark, confident and plain. Three colours only: ink, paper and one red. Very large, heavy, condensed headlines in sentence case, with quiet body text beside or under them. Real photographs of people mid-action, used big: as the whole background of a slide or as a framed object, never as decoration. Flat paper cards with a hairline edge and a soft shadow. Generous empty space, one idea to a slide. No gradients, no second accent colour, no clip art, no icons as ornament, no drop caps, no slide furniture beyond a small wordmark and a page number.

## 1. Slide setup

- Size: 16:9, 13.333 by 7.5 inches.
- Margins: 0.6in on all four sides. Nothing but a full-bleed background or photograph crosses them.
- Grid: 12 columns across the 12.13in between the margins, each 0.81in wide with a 0.22in gutter. Place every block on column edges.
- Every slide: the wordmark at the top left (0.6in from the left, 0.42in from the top, 0.24in tall) and the page number at the bottom right (11pt, secondary text colour). Content starts 1.2in from the top and ends 6.7in from the top. A title slide and a closing slide may drop the page number.
- Remove what PowerPoint adds by default: no shape shadows, outlines or blue fills you did not choose, no automatic bullets, no "click to add" placeholders left on a finished slide, no autofit shrinking text.

### Backgrounds

A slide's background is ink or paper, full bleed. Ink is the brand's home: open and close a deck on ink, and let ink carry at least half the slides. Alternate ink and paper for pace instead of changing layouts for the sake of it. Two special backgrounds exist: well, for a slide of paper cards, and red, for a single statement slide. Use a red background at most once in a deck.

| Background | Text | Secondary text | Hairlines |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ink | Paper | Paper soft | `#474748` |
| Paper | Ink | Ink soft | Hairline |
| Well | Ink | Ink soft | `#CCCCCD` |
| Red | Paper | Paper | `#DE7989` |
| A photograph | Paper, over a scrim (section 6) | Paper soft | none |

### Using red

Red is rare, which is why it works. On any one slide it appears in one or two places, chosen from: a filled button-like label with paper text, a short rule, a small square mark, the one bar, pill, row or card that the slide is about, a numeral in a numbered sequence, and the brand's own name inside a headline.

- **Red is never text on ink.** Red on ink measures 2.95:1, below the 3:1 minimum. On ink, red is a filled shape, a rule or a mark. The single exception: the words "AthleteOS" and "AOS" inside a headline are set in red on any background.
- Red text on paper is allowed (5.63:1) but is for a numeral or a single word, never a paragraph.
- Red is never a gradient, never a tint, never a background behind body text other than the one statement slide.

Contrast for reference: paper on ink 16.63:1, paper soft on ink 8.70:1, ink soft on paper 6.55:1, paper on red 5.63:1.

## 3. Type

### Typefaces

Two, and only two. The files are in `fonts/` and must be installed on the machine that makes and shows the deck.

| Use | Typeface | Name to select in PowerPoint |
|---|---|---|
| The largest headlines | Big Shoulders Display, ExtraBold (800) | `Big Shoulders Display ExtraBold` |
| Other headings | Big Shoulders Display, Bold (700) | `Big Shoulders Display` with Bold on |
| Body text | Barlow, Regular (400) | `Barlow` |
| Labels and emphasis | Barlow, Medium (500) | `Barlow Medium` |

No italics, no underlined emphasis, no all-caps headings, no other weights, no third typeface, no monospace. If the fonts cannot be installed, say so instead of substituting silently; Arial Narrow Bold for headings and Arial for body are the closest stand-ins and are not the brand.

### Sizes

| Style | Typeface | Size | Line spacing (exactly) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Big Shoulders Display ExtraBold | 80pt | 84pt | A title slide, a divider, a closing slide |
| Heading 1 | Big Shoulders Display ExtraBold | 54pt | 57pt | A slide's headline |
| Heading 2 | Big Shoulders Display Bold | 36pt | 38pt | A long headline, a quote |
| Heading 3 | Big Shoulders Display Bold | 24pt | 26pt | A name over a paragraph; a closing statement |
| Heading 4 | Big Shoulders Display Bold | 18pt | 19pt | A name that stands alone or labels a picture |
| Lead | Barlow | 18pt | 28pt | The sentence or two that opens a slide, in the main text colour |
| Body | Barlow | 14pt | 22pt | Paragraphs, usually in the secondary colour |
| Small | Barlow | 11pt | 17pt | Captions, footnotes, the page number |
| Label | Barlow Medium | 14pt | 18pt | Button-like labels, attributions, row titles |
| Interface | Barlow Medium | 11pt, 9pt for pills | 14pt, 12pt | Text inside a panel that depicts product interface |
| Mega | Big Shoulders Display ExtraBold | 120 to 180pt | same as size | One numeral or one word used as an image. Never a sentence |

These are the website's desktop sizes scaled to a slide, with body raised to 14pt so it reads on a screen share. Both faces are condensed, so they are set larger than a normal-width face would be. Do not shrink them to "normal" slide sizes, and do not let PowerPoint autofit them.

### Setting type

- **Headlines are sentences.** Sentence case, ending in a full stop, usually two or three lines. Never title case, never all caps.
- **Line spacing for headings is 1.05 times the size and never tighter.** Tighter and the descenders of one line touch the next.
- **Letter spacing is normal (0).** Never tighten or expand a condensed face.
- **Left aligned** by default, ragged right, never justified. Centre a slide's text only when the whole composition is symmetrical (a statement slide, a closing slide).
- **Line length.** A body paragraph is at most about 6.5in wide (seven columns). A headline may run wider.
- **Hierarchy on a slide:** one headline, at most one lead, then the content. The lead is the main text colour; supporting paragraphs are the secondary colour. Never mute the lead, never make body text bold to shout.
- **The brand name in a headline is red.** "AthleteOS" and "AOS" inside a heading are set in red; in body text they are the body colour.
- **Hollow type.** A large numeral or a single word may be drawn as an outline with no fill (text fill none, text outline 1.5 to 2.5pt in the hollow outline colour, or paper when it sits on a photograph). Use it once on a slide at most, at Mega or Display size.
- **No label above a heading.** No kicker, eyebrow, tag or category line above a headline or a card title, and never a numbered one such as "01" or "02 / 04". A numeral may be part of a design, as the red ordinal of a numbered card, never a label sitting above a heading.

## 4. Layout

- **One idea to a slide.** If a slide needs more than a headline, a lead and one group of content, it is two slides.
- **The standard opening:** the headline on the left across seven columns, the lead on the right across the last four or five, both starting 1.2in from the top. Or stack them: headline, then lead under it at paragraph width.
- **Spacing steps.** Use only these distances: 0.08in inside a control, 0.22in between elements of one group, 0.7in between groups, 0.6in at the slide's edges. Inside a card, 0.25 to 0.55in of padding depending on the card's size.
- **Alignment.** Everything sits on a column edge and shares left edges with what is above it. In a card or a row, the title and the text under it start on the same left edge.
- **Empty space is part of the design.** Do not fill a slide. A headline alone on ink is a finished slide.
- **Make consecutive slides differ in composition**, not in colour alone: change where the weight sits, how dense the slide is, and whether a picture or type leads.

## 5. Shapes and surfaces

### Corners

One scale, and the size of the corner follows the size of the thing. PowerPoint's rounded rectangle sets its corner as a proportion, so adjust the handle until the radius is the size given.

| Thing | Corner radius |
|---|---|
| Small marks, the seat mark | 0.02in |
| Buttons, chips, icon tiles | 0.075in |
| Cards and panels, at any size | 0.11in |
| Framed photographs and large cards | 0.15in |
| Soft objects, such as an app tile | 0.22in |
| Pills and progress bars | fully round |

A background photograph and a full-bleed band have square corners. When one rounded thing sits inside another, the inner corner is the outer corner less the padding between them.

### Lines

Every line is a hairline, 0.75pt, in the hairline colour for its background. Use lines to divide rows and to tie a set together, not to box things in. The one heavier line is a short red rule, 1.5pt.

### Cards

A card is a flat rounded rectangle with a paper fill, a 0.75pt edge in the card edge colour, and the medium shadow. It stands on paper, on well, or on ink and photographs, where its text is ink like any paper surface. No gradient, no coloured border, no header band. In a set of cards, one may be filled red with paper text to mark the last or the most important.

A panel that depicts product interface (a list of rows, a dashboard, a notification) is always paper with ink text, whatever the slide's background, with a hairline border and the extra-large shadow. Its rows are divided by hairlines, its text is the interface size, and it is labelled "Sample" when it shows figures that are not real.

### Shadows

Shadows are ink, soft and directly below (angle 90 degrees). They are strong on ink and over photographs, and under half that strength on paper, where a strong shadow reads as dirt.

| Step | Blur | Distance | Transparency on paper | Transparency on ink or a photograph | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | 11pt | 5pt | 92% | 82% | A card on the slide's background |
| Large | 24pt | 12pt | 83% | 62% | A framed photograph |
| Extra large | 43pt | 21pt | 75% | 45% | A panel floating over a photograph or ink |

No glow, no reflection, no bevel, no 3D, no inner shadow.

### Small parts

- **Button-like label:** a red rounded rectangle (0.075in corner) at least 0.45in tall with a Barlow Medium label in paper, 0.3in of padding either side. Beside it, a secondary action is plain Barlow Medium text with a 1.5pt red underline set a little below it, not a second button.
- **State pill:** fully round, 0.24in tall, 9pt Barlow Medium. The one thing that is due or matters is red with paper text; a thing in progress is an outline with no fill; everything else is a quiet fill with secondary text.
- **The seat mark:** the brand's small repeated mark, a red rounded rectangle 0.82 as tall as it is wide (for example 0.13 by 0.107in, corner 0.02in). It stands for one seat in a stadium. Use it wherever a bullet, a separator dot, a step marker or a "you are here" mark is needed. It is the only bullet the brand has.
- **Lists.** Never PowerPoint's default bullets. A list is either rows divided by hairlines, each a heading-face name with an optional line of body text, or short items each preceded by a seat mark. To single out one item of a list: full-strength text, a seat mark before it and a red rule under it, with the other names at `#7F7F7F` on paper or paper soft on ink.
- **Numbered sequences:** the numeral in red Heading 3 in its own narrow column, so the name and the text under it share one left edge. The last card may be red, with its numeral in paper.
- **Steps in a process:** columns tied by one hairline across the top, a seat mark sitting on the line at the start of each column, then the step's name in Heading 4 and its text in body.
- **Quotes:** Heading 2 on a paper card, a large red opening quotation mark breaking the card's top edge, the key phrase highlighted with the marker colour, then a hairline and the attribution in Barlow Medium after a short red rule.
- **Icons:** thin single-colour line icons only, in the text colour, 0.15 to 0.2in, used to identify a row or a kind of thing. Never as decoration, never in red, never filled or multi-colour. No emoji.

### Charts and tables

- **Charts** are flat: no 3D, no gradient, no shadow, no border, no background fill. Bars and lines are the slide's text colour, and the single series or bar the slide is about is red. Further series use ink soft, paper soft and hairline, never new colours. Gridlines are hairlines or absent. Label data directly instead of using a legend where possible. Axis and data labels are Barlow 11pt in the secondary colour. One chart to a slide, with a headline that states what it shows.
- **Tables** have no fills and no vertical lines: rows divided by hairlines, a header row in Barlow Medium in the secondary colour, numbers right aligned. Mark the one row or cell that matters with red text on paper, or with a red pill.
- Any figure that is illustrative says so on the slide.

## 6. Photography

### What the pictures are

- Real photographs of real places, or pictures indistinguishable from them: an arena tunnel, a game in progress, a boardroom, a networking event. Each looks as if the photographer who would really be there took it, in that place's real light.
- People doing something, never posing and never looking into the camera. In a group, each person is doing something different.
- Serious, warm and confident. Nobody looks sad, lonely or defeated.
- Varied in subject and distance across a deck: a close portrait, a wide arena, a mid-distance meeting. The same motif repeated is a failure.
- Straight out of the camera: natural colour, visible texture, no retouching, no glamour, no heavy grade, no haze or lens flare. Avoid anything that reads as "studio", "polished" or "cinematic", and anything that reads as stock.
- Sports shown are basketball, American football, golf, ice hockey and baseball, at professional or NCAA Division I level.
- No readable text, interface or lit screens inside a picture.
- Never a generated or altered picture of a real, named person. Never a stock photograph of a stranger presented as an AthleteOS client or team member. Never clip art, illustration, 3D renders or abstract gradient art.

### Two ways to use a picture

| | A background photograph | A framed photograph |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The whole slide, or a band running edge to edge | An object placed on the slide |
| Corners | Square | 0.15in |
| Edge | None | A 0.75pt paper line at 84% transparency |
| Shadow | None | Large |
| Words on it | Yes, over a scrim, in the picture's quiet area | Optional: a name and one sentence at its foot, over a scrim |

Crop a picture to fill its box completely and keep the main subject, usually a face, whole and clear of the wordmark, of any text and of any card laid over it. Never stretch a picture. Never tilt, rotate or mask a picture into a circle or any shape but a rectangle.

### The house look

Every photograph gets one of two looks, so pictures from different places sit together. Apply it with Picture Format, or before inserting the picture.

- **Colour, the default:** slightly muted and slightly warm. Approximately saturation 86%, contrast +6%, brightness -3%, and a touch of warmth. (The website's exact filter is `saturate(0.86) contrast(1.06) brightness(0.97) sepia(0.08)`.)
- **Black and white, on purpose:** saturation 0%, contrast +6%. Use it for a picture that sits behind a card or text and should stay quiet. Do not mix the two looks on one slide by accident.

### Words over a picture

Text never sits directly on a photograph. Lay a scrim between them: a rectangle the size of the picture, filled with a linear gradient of ink that fades to fully transparent, with no outline.

- **Text at the foot of a picture:** dark at the bottom. Ink at 0% transparency at the bottom edge, 22% transparency about a sixth of the way up, fully transparent just under halfway up.
- **Text on the left of a wide picture:** dark at the left. Ink at 18% transparency at the left edge, 54% a third of the way across, fully transparent at about 58%.
- **The wordmark over the top of a picture:** ink at 25% transparency at the top edge fading to fully transparent within the top fifth.

Choose or crop the picture so the text sits over a plain, dim, low-contrast area with no faces or highlights. On a picture, text is paper and secondary text is paper soft.

### Interface over a picture

A chat thread, a dashboard, a statistic, a notification or a phone screen is never part of a picture. Use a clean photograph and build what sits on top from shapes and live text, as a paper panel (section 5). That keeps it sharp, editable and on-brand.

## 7. The logo

The files are in `logo/`. The wordmark is "Athlete" in the text colour and "OS" in red.

| File | Use on |
|---|---|
| `athleteos-logo-on-ink.png` (or `.svg`) | Ink backgrounds and photographs |
| `athleteos-logo-on-paper.png` (or `athleteos-logo.svg`) | Paper and well backgrounds |
| `athleteos-logo-one-colour-paper.png` (or `.svg`) | A red background, where the red "OS" would vanish |
| `athleteos-mark.png` (or `.svg`) | The "AOS" monogram on an ink tile, for an avatar or app-icon spot only |

- Standard size is 0.24in tall at the top left of every slide. It may be used once at large size, as wide as the content area (12.13in) across the foot of a closing slide.
- Recolour only as the files do. Never redraw, retype, stretch, rotate, outline or add an effect to it, and never place it on a busy part of a photograph without a scrim.
- Never set the company's name as live text where the logo belongs. In running text the company is written "AthleteOS", one word, capital A, capital O and S.

## 8. Words on a slide

- Plain, direct, second person: "you" and "your". Short declarative sentences. A headline says the point of the slide; it is not a topic label.
- No em dashes. Rewrite the sentence, or use a comma, a colon or a full stop. A hyphen or an en dash in the same place is not a fix.
- No exclamation marks, no rhetorical hype, no buzzword stacks.
- Never invent a client, a name, a logo, a number, a testimonial or a result to fill a slide. If content is missing, say what is missing; if a figure is illustrative, label it "Sample".

## 9. Animation and transitions

The website moves a great deal, but always from one calm vocabulary, and a slide should take the same restraint. Use no slide transition, or Fade. If elements build, use Fade or a short Wipe, about half a second, in reading order, started on click. Nothing bounces, spins, flies in or zooms, and no animation repeats. A deck must read completely with animation turned off or exported to PDF.

## 10. Before handing a slide over

1. Only ink, paper, red and the listed greys are on the slide. No stray blues, no pure black or white.
2. Only Big Shoulders Display and Barlow, at sizes from the table, with headings at 1.05 line spacing and no autofit.
3. One headline, in sentence case with a full stop, and no label above it.
4. Red appears in one or two places and is never text on ink, except the brand name in a headline.
5. Everything sits inside 0.6in margins and on the grid, with left edges shared.
6. Cards have the right corner, a hairline edge and the right shadow for their background. Nothing carries a default PowerPoint style.
7. Every photograph is real-looking, fills its box, has the house look, keeps its subject clear, and has a scrim under any words. Framed pictures have their corner, hairline and shadow; background pictures have none.
8. The wordmark is the right file for the background, at the top left, undistorted.
9. No em dashes, no invented facts, sample figures labelled.
10. Text contrast holds: body text is never ink soft on ink or paper soft on paper, and nothing readable sits on a photograph without a scrim.
11. Every picture has alt text, and the slide's reading order is headline, lead, content.
